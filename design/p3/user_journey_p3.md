# User Journey

### Trigger

Jane is a college senior who couldn't put a book down in middle school, but hasn't finished one
outside of class in four years. Over winter break she goes to her local library and is
overwhelmed by the shelves: she no longer knows what she likes. The librarian's suggestions sit
half-read on her desk, and Reddit threads keep recommending the same handful of books she read
years ago. A friend suggests Novelty, and Jane opens it that evening.

### Step 1: Creating an account

Novelty opens on a sign-in screen, because everything in the app is personal to the reader: her
feed, her shelves, and what she cares about in a book. Jane chooses **Create account**, picks a
username and a password of at least 8 characters, and goes straight to her feed.

### Step 2: Telling Novelty what matters to her

The first First Impression card already shows the book's cover, summary, setting, themes, tones
and characters. Where the radar chart would be, though, it says "Weight the criteria you care
about below to see them here." Jane taps the **profile icon** in the top right corner.

Her profile shows that she's signed in, and it lists **Your criteria**: contemplative, dystopian,
introspective, pacing and social-commentary, each with a short description and a weight from 0
to 5. She remembers loving *The Giver*, so she sets **social-commentary to 5**, **dystopian to 4**,
**introspective to 3** and **contemplative to 2**. She leaves **pacing at 0**, since a slow book
doesn't bother her. Each change saves as soon as she makes it ("Saved: social-commentary is now
5."). The whole setup takes under a minute.

These weights do two things. They decide which criteria appear on the radar of every card she
sees, and they push books that are strong on those criteria toward the top of her feed.

### Step 3: Swiping through her feed

Jane taps **Novelty** in the middle of the top bar to return to her feed. Each card now has a
radar chart with just her four criteria, so she can see at a glance how a book matches what she
asked for.

Her feed leads with the books that fit her best. *Brave New World* comes first, scoring high on
social commentary and dystopian, and she reads its spoiler-free summary and characters. She
**drags the card to the right** (she could also press **Up Next ♥** or the right arrow key), and
the app confirms "Added Brave New World to Up Next." A few cards later comes *The Hobbit*, whose
radar is nearly flat on her criteria, and she drags it left to pass.

Each next card is ready the moment she swipes, already fully loaded with its cover and radar,
because Novelty prepares the next few cards in the background. Every swipe also teaches the app
about her: liking *Brave New World* nudges up the criteria it is strong on, and passing on *The
Hobbit* nudges down the ones it is strong on. As she keeps swiping, the order of her feed adjusts
to both what she told it and how she swipes.

### Step 4: The feed keeps going

Novelty starts with a small catalog, and Jane gets through it quickly. She doesn't hit a wall,
though. When she has 10 or fewer unseen books left, Novelty starts finding more for her in the
background: it pulls about 20 new books from Open Library in the genres of the books she liked
(or, before she has liked any, the genres across the whole catalog), and writes each one's First
Impression card (summary, characters, themes, tones and scores on
every criterion). A book only joins the feed once its card is complete, so Jane never sees a card
with missing information.

In a few minutes, new titles in the genres she's been liking appear in her feed, ranked by how
well they fit her. If she ever swipes faster than the new books arrive, the feed shows "Finding
your next book…" until they're ready.

### Step 5: Looking through her shelves

Jane taps the **shelf icon** in the top left corner. Her shelves open on **Up Next**, which lists
every book she swiped right on. She decides *Brave New World* will be her next read, and checks it
out of the library the next day.

She also opens the **Passed** tab, which lists the books she swiped left on. Reading the
list, she second-guesses passing on *Never Let Me Go*, presses **Reconsider**, and it goes back
into her feed so she can take another look.

### Step 6: Finishing a book and choosing the next one

A week and a half later, Jane finishes *Brave New World* and loves it. On her Up Next shelf she
presses **Finished: add to Favorites**, which moves it from Up Next to her **Favorites** tab. She
also realizes she cares more about introspective books than she thought, so she raises
**introspective to 5** in her profile. Her feed reshuffles straight away, and every card's radar
now reflects the change.

Instead of dreading what to read next, she either opens her feed for fresh suggestions or picks
from the books already waiting on her Up Next shelf.

### Outcome

Jane never has to stand in front of a library shelf feeling lost again. The app didn't choose her
books for her. It let her say what she values, showed her clearly how each book measures up on
exactly those things, and kept bringing her new books that match. Within a month she has finished
two books and started a third.

### What changed since the MVP

- **Accounts.** Jane signs up and signs in, so her feed, shelves and criteria are her own.
- **Her own criteria.** She weights the criteria in her profile. The weights choose what her radar
  charts show and shape the ranking of her feed, alongside what the app learns from her swipes.
- **Real swiping.** Cards can be dragged left or right, or swiped with the buttons or arrow keys,
  and each card is fully loaded before it appears.
- **A feed that doesn't run out.** New books are found and written up automatically when she's
  close to the end.
- **Separate pages.** The feed is the main page, with her shelves (top left) and profile (top
  right) one tap away. The shelves include a **Passed** tab for reconsidering books.
- **Not built yet.** Following friends and browsing their shelves still needs to be implemented and the criteria that the user can rank in their profile needs to be broadened.
