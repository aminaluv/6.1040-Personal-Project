# User Journey

### Trigger
Jane is a college senior who loved to read when they were in middle school, they used to be unable to put down a book, but since then, Jane hasn't fully finished a book in four years other than the books that were assigned for class homework or projects. Over her winter break, she decides to check out her local library to see if she could find a book that interested her. Once she enters the library, she is immediately overwhelmed by the sheer number of options. Jane realizes that she doesn't even know what books interest her anymore. She tries asking the librarian for suggestions and picks out a couple books. However, after a couple days, Jane just starts the books and never actually finishes them. Jane tries looking on Reddit for book suggestions but a lot of the books she had already read when she was younger and these forums seem to be repeating the same handful of books that don't interest her. Jane talks with her friend about the problem she is having, and her friend suggests using the app Novelty. Intrigued, Jane downloads the app. 

### Step 1: Building a Reader Profile (Sketch 2: Reader Profile)
After downloading the app, the app requires her to make an account for herself, so she chooses a username and password. Then the app asks a few questions about what criteria Jane uses to determine whether a book is a good book. She remembers loving "The Giver" when she was younger, so she adds it as a past favorite and picks her criteria to be contemplative, introspective, dystopian, and social commentary books. Jane notices that there is also an option to continue without inputting her criteria if she is a new reader, but since she has an idea of what she used to like, she decided to continue with her inputted criteria for now. She weighs social commentary as the highest criteria since she wants books that get her to think about society as a whole. The whole setup takes about 3 minutes. 

### Step 2: Swiping through her feed and Deciding with a First Impression card (Sketch 1: Reader Feed)
The app then leads her to her feed where she is instructed to swipe right if she likes a book and left otherwise. The app then shows her one book at a time, the app calls each book card a First Impression card. The first book is a fantasy adventure book, the radar chart shows that is scores low on social commentary and contemplative. She reads through the spoiler-free summary, characters, themes, and tones. She decides it doesn't pique her interest and she swipes left on this book. A few swipes later the app shows her the book "Brave New World". She decides to swipe right on this book, and the app makes it easy to see that the book is a good match with the radar chart ranking this book high in social commentary. As Jane continues to swipe through the First Impression cards, she notices that the books are getting closer and closer to books that truly interest her. 

### Step 3: Looking through her Up Next Shelf (Sketch 4: Your Shelf)
After swiping through a good number of books, Jane decides to take a look at her shelf. When she clicks on her shelf she sees a list of books marked as "Up Next" which were all the books that she swiped right on. From this list, she decides that "Brave New World" will be her next read. She then decides to go to the library the next day, and checks out "Brave New World". After a week and a half, she finishes the book! She really liked the book and feels like she has a better understanding of what she is looking for in a new book. 

### Step 4: Seeing what friends are reading (Sketch 3, Sketch 5, Sketch 6)
Jane gets a follow request from her friend Maya that had suggested the app Novelty to her. She decides to take a look at the books that Maya is interested in and notices that a Maya's favorites shelf only contains Kazuo Ishiguro books. Jane hadn't known this about Maya and they talk about Kazuo Ishiguro the next time they chat. Jane found out that Maya really liked the book "Klara and the Sun", Jane decided to add this book to her Up Next shelf. 

### Step 5: Favoriting books and choose your next read (Sketch 4: Your Shelf)
Since Jane finished "Brave New World", she decides to add this book to her list of favorites since she really liked it. This helps the app to sharpen Jane's feed and future book suggestions. Instead of the familiar dread of having to choose her next book, she opens up her Up Next list and finds "Klara and the Sun" already waiting. 

### Outcome
Jane never has to stand in front of a library shelf feeling lost and overwhelmed again. Within a month, Jane was able to finish two new books and start a third book. She and Maya have been having a running conversation about Ishiguro. The app didn't pick her books for her, instead it gave her clear, comprehensive information about each book so that she can be exposed to books that match her criteria. She can finally pick up new books with confidence.
